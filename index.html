What these mean
CAPE
Fuel for thunderstorms — how much energy a rising air parcel gains (J/kg). 500+ supports storms, 1,000–2,500 strong storms, 2,500+ explosive.
CIN
The “cap” holding storms back (J/kg). Near 0 means storms can fire freely; below −100 means a strong cap.
Lifted index
Instability at ~5.5 km. Negative is unstable: −3 to −6 strong, below −6 extreme.
0–6 km shear
Change in wind with height. Organizes storms: 35+ kt supports supercells, 50+ kt strongly.
0–1 km shear
Low-level wind change — key for tornadoes. 20+ kt is notable.
SRH 0–1 / 0–3 km
Storm-relative helicity: how much spin a storm can ingest (m²/s²). 0–1 km 100+ and 0–3 km 150+ favour rotating storms; 0–1 km 200+ is strongly tornadic.
LCL height
Cloud-base height. Below ~1,000 m favours tornadoes; above 2,000 m they're unlikely.
STP
Significant Tornado Parameter — combines CAPE, LCL, SRH, shear and CIN. Above 1 signals significant (EF2+) tornado environments.
SCP
Supercell Composite Parameter. Above 1 supports supercells; above 4 strongly.
Lapse rate
Temperature drop from 700 to 500 hPa (°C/km). 7+ is steep and boosts hail and updraft strength.
K index
Air-mass thunderstorm potential. 30+ scattered storms, 40+ numerous.
Hodograph
Wind at each height plotted as a curve (red 0–1 km, green 1–3, yellow 3–6, blue 6–9). Long, curved hodographs mean rotating storms. RM is the predicted right-moving supercell motion.
